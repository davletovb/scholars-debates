    About this timeline

    Each star is a scholar, placed in the lane of their main discipline at the middle of their life. The bars at the top are the debates they argued over, spanning the years each debate was most active. Faint threads join each debate to the scholars who took part in it.

    Select a scholar to see the places of their life on the globe, in order. Select a debate to follow where it was argued, year by year. With nothing selected, the globe shows where the scholars in view lived, and turns as you move through the centuries.

    Positions in each debate are short editorial summaries, not quotations. The scholars listed under a position are associated with it: some argued it directly, others developed or defended it later, and some stand for their school. On the globe, a debate’s lines join the places where it was argued in the order of the timeline; they do not trace documented routes of transmission. A scholar’s route shows selected places from the sources, in order, including returns.

    Dates are shown in both the Common Era and the Hijri calendar; a “c.” marks an approximate year, and Hijri years worked out from Common Era dates are marked the same way. Where a birth year is unknown or disputed, the scholar’s panel says so, and any year used only to draw the life line is marked as an estimate. The timeline runs from 610 to 1988, the latest death of a scholar in the dataset; the last dated debate entry is from 1980.

    What still needs work

    This is a first version with 99 scholars and 18 debates. Each scholar, debate and event lists its sources at the end of its panel, mostly entries in Britannica, Encyclopaedia Iranica, the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Oxford Reference and the TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi (in Turkish), with specialist studies where a date or attribution is disputed. Every scholar has at least one source in English. Dated debate entries that go beyond those sources cite their own. Ibadi and Isma’ili scholars, Southeast Asia, Africa beyond Sokoto and more women scholars are still to be added.

    The map

    Region names on the globe are historical: Mawara’ al-nahr, Khurasan, al-Andalus. Coastlines, rivers and lakes are modern, from Natural Earth (public domain), except the Aral Sea and Lake Chad, which are drawn as fixed outlines of their historical extent. City positions are approximate, and a few places, such as Jami’s birthplace in the district of Jam, are marked at the district rather than the village.

    Keyboard

    Tab to any star or debate and press Enter to open it. Use the arrow keys in the timeline to move through time, and Escape to close a panel.
